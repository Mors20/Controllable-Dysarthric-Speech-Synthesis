Controllable Dysarthric Speech Synthesis with Patient-Specific Conditioning for Speaker-Diverse ASR Augmentation

Code

Abstract Dysarthric speech recognition remains challenging because dysarthric speech is highly variable across speakers and only limited labeled training data are available. Although speech synthesis has been explored for ASR data augmentation, existing methods often couple speaker identity with dysarthric articulation, limiting the controllability of the generated data. We propose a controllable dysarthric speech synthesis framework for ASR data augmentation that uses separate prompt-derived timbre conditioning prefixes and learnable patient-specific pathology conditioning prefixes. Built on a pre-trained neural codec language model adapted with lightweight LoRA, the framework combines the two prefixes through additive conditioning. A dual-classifier objective with gradient reversal and VC-based counterfactual augmentation are used to promote factor separation between timbre-related and pathology-related information. This design supports pairing a learned patient-specific pathology condition with different target prompt speakers, including healthy speakers. Experiments on the TORGO dataset show that the generated speech can partially substitute for real dysarthric training data and provides effective speaker-diverse augmentation when combined with real data. Objective ASR evaluations, perceptual evaluations, factor-separation analysis, and phoneme-level error analysis provide evidence that the proposed conditioning strategy preserves target-speaker timbre while producing pathology-dependent patterns consistent with real dysarthric speech.
